4-day Upper / Lower

Each muscle group ~2×/week — the sweet spot for growth. The principle that matters most: progressive overload. When you hit the top of a rep range on all sets, add weight (~2.5–5 lb upper, 5–10 lb lower). If a shift eats a session, run it as a 3-day week.

Trap-bar deadlifts and loaded carries are gold here — they train the exact hinge, lift, and carry the job demands. Deload every 6–8 weeks: one lighter week at ~50% of normal sets.

Conditioning sessions

Zone 2 · 30–45 min
Easy incline walk (vest optional), bike, row, ruck. Talking pace. Builds the engine that recovers you between calls. Lowest interference with lifting.
Fireground circuit · 20–30 min
4–6 rounds, 3–4 of: weighted stair climb, sled/sandbag drag, farmer carry, sledgehammer strikes, sandbag clean+carry, battle ropes. Maps onto CPAT events.
Intervals · ~15 min (optional)
8–10 × 30s hard / 90s easy on bike or rower. Keep hard work ≤12 min total.
Keep cardio off running where you can — bike/row/incline interfere less with muscle and pound the joints less. Separate hard cardio from lifting by 6+ hrs, or do it after lifting, never before legs.

Shifts rotate across the calendar, so anchor key lifts to OFF days, use the station gym on quiet ON days, and treat the job itself as conditioning. A representative 8-day cycle:

The honest part

Losing 10 lb of fat and gaining 20 lb of muscle at once is possible, but mainly for people who are new to lifting, returning after a layoff, or carrying extra fat. Muscle gain wants a surplus; fat loss wants a deficit — recomposition threads the needle and works best in that early window.

If you're already lean and experienced, 20 lb of new muscle is a multi-year project. Phase it instead: a 12–16 week lean bulk to build, then an 8–12 week cut to reveal it. Set your goal lead on the Dashboard accordingly.

Muscle is slow either way. Plan in months, judge progress every 4 weeks, and ignore day-to-day scale noise. This is general fitness information, not medical advice — get cleared by a doctor if you have any health conditions.

Nutrition

Protein every meal: chicken, lean beef, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, whey. Hitting ~1 g/lb is the biggest food lever for the muscle goal.

Carbs are fuel, not the enemy — you do hard physical work: rice, potatoes, oats, fruit, whole grains, beans. Fats from olive oil, nuts, avocado, fatty fish.

Hydration: ~0.5–1 oz water per lb bodyweight baseline, more around heat. After any working fire: water + electrolytes first, then protein + carbs.

Eating on a 24-hour shift — calls don't care about meal timing, so pack a cooler and don't outsource nutrition to the group dinner:

  • Pre-cooked protein + pre-portioned rice/potato containers
  • Greek yogurt or cottage cheese, fruit, jerky, nuts, rice cakes
  • 1–2 whey shakes — your bail-out protein when a call hits mid-meal
  • If dinner's pizza, eat it, then add a shake to hit your number
  • Caffeine early; moderate the alcohol (it wrecks recovery and sleep)

Sample non-shift day: 4 eggs + oats + berries · 8 oz chicken + rice + veg · yogurt + banana pre-train · 8 oz beef or salmon + potato + salad · cottage cheese before bed.

Recovery & sleep

Muscle is built during recovery, and shift work is the biggest threat to it. Treat sleep as part of the program.

  • On shift: sleep whenever volume allows, even broken. Protect a dark, quiet bunk window; cut caffeine after midday.
  • Post-24: a 20–90 min nap the next morning clears the debt, then a normal night to reset.
  • Off days: 7–9 hrs with a consistent wake time to anchor your clock.
  • The job is high-cortisol on its own — don't bury yourself under excessive training too. Take a real rest day each week.
Supplements (optional, evidence-backed)
  • Creatine monohydrate — 5 g/day, every day. Most proven supplement for strength and muscle.
  • Whey protein — convenience to hit your protein target.
  • Caffeine — pre-training (mind your shift-sleep timing).
  • Electrolytes — around working fires and hard sessions.
  • Vitamin D — if you're low (worth a blood test).

Nothing exotic required. Calories, protein, progressive overload, and sleep do ~95% of the work.
